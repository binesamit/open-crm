Bines OS is a flexible work platform where every team builds the tools it needs: client tracking, project management, handling requests, hiring and more. Instead of rigid software with fixed screens, you put together boards, columns, views and automations — and the platform adapts to the way you work. It's worth spending a few minutes on the overall structure, because almost everything else follows from it: where information is kept, who sees it and how to find it quickly.

## What you'll learn

- What the platform's building blocks are and how they fit inside each other
- What the account's home screen shows
- What the interface looks like on a computer and on a phone
- Where to start with your first steps

## The building blocks

The platform is built in layers, from the widest to the narrowest:

| Layer | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Account | Your organization. Each account has its own users, settings and plan | "Noga Studio Ltd." |
| Workspace | A large area that brings together the work of a department or field | "Sales", "Operations" |
| Folder | A way to arrange boards inside a workspace (up to three levels of folders and subfolders) | "Clients 2026" |
| Board | The work table itself — where you manage the information | "Leads", "Active projects" |
| Group | A section of the board, for example by stage or month | "New", "In progress", "Closed" |
| Item | A row on the board — a client, task, request or anything else | "Alpha Inc." |
| Subitem | A sub-item under an item, to break a task into steps | "Send a quote" |

Every board has **columns** that define what information is kept for each item (status, owner, date, number, files and more), and **views** that show the same information in different ways — table, Kanban, calendar, timeline, chart and more. Besides boards, a workspace can also hold **docs** and **dashboards**.

> **Tip:** You can change almost everything from the interface — names, columns, status labels, views and permissions. You don't need a developer to fit the platform to your process.

## The home screen

After signing in you'll reach the account's home screen, headed **Welcome to** and the account's name. What it shows depends on your role:

1. **Workspaces** — the list of workspaces you can see. Clicking a workspace opens its page, with its description, folders and boards. A closed workspace is marked with a **Closed workspace** label.
2. **Invite users** — a short form for inviting colleagues to the account (for those allowed to invite).
3. Account admins also see an **Administration** card with shortcuts to users, teams, templates and connections, and an **Open administration** button.
4. Other users see the list of **Users** in the account, **Teams** and the available templates.

## The interface on a computer

The screen is split in two: the **sidebar** on one side, and the main content area next to it.

- At the top of the sidebar are the account's name and your role.
- Below it is a row of quick icons: **Home**, **My work**, **Inbox**, **Automations**, search, the help center and the notifications bell.
- Further down — **Favorites**, followed by the **Workspace** tree with its folders, boards, docs and dashboards.
- At the bottom — your avatar, which opens your personal menu (profile, appearance, switch account and sign out).

## The interface on a phone

On a phone the sidebar becomes a drawer that opens from the menu button, and a navigation bar with five tabs appears at the bottom of the screen: **Home**, **My work**, **Updates**, **Boards** and **Me**. The **Boards** tab shows the workspace tree full screen, and the **Me** tab brings together your profile, settings, switching accounts and signing out.

## Recommended first steps

1. Complete your profile — name, photo and job title — so colleagues recognize you.
2. Choose a workspace (or create a new one) and open your first board in it.
3. Adjust the columns and groups to your process.
4. Invite the people you work with.
5. Add your important boards to Favorites, so you can reach them in one click.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the difference between a workspace and a folder?**\
A workspace is a large unit with its own access settings (open or closed) and member list. A folder is just a way of arranging things inside a workspace, and doesn't change who sees what.

**I'm a member of several accounts. Is information shared between them?**\
No. Each account is completely separate. You can move between them from the avatar menu with **Switch account**.

**Why can't I see a board my colleague can see?**\
Visibility depends on your role in the account, the type of workspace and the board's permissions. You'll find full details in the articles on roles and permissions.
